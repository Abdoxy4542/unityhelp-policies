UnityHelp Mobile - Privacy Policy

Last Updated: November 20, 2026
Effective Date: November 20, 2026

1. Who we are and what this covers

UnityHelp Mobile ("the App") is the field app of the UnityHelp platform. Aid organisations use it to report from the places where they work: gathering sites, community kitchens (takayas), health centres, distribution points, nutrition centres and protection areas.

This policy explains what the App collects, why, where it is kept, who can see it, and what you can ask for. It covers the App only. The UnityHelp website has its own sign-in and is described where it differs.

Who decides about the data. Each organisation that uses UnityHelp decides what its field workers collect and what happens to it. UnityHelp runs the platform for those organisations. If you are a field worker, your organisation is your first contact for any question about your data or the data you collect.

Contact: info@unityhelp.org

2. Who can use the App

The App can be installed by anyone, but only field workers can sign in. A field worker's account is opened by their organisation's administrator. Nobody can register in the App.

The App is meant for adults working for an aid organisation. It is not intended for anyone under 18.

3. What the App collects

3.1 Your account

Your organisation records these when it opens your account:

  • Your full name and phone number. You sign in with your phone number and a PIN.
  • Your organisation, the kind of place you work at, the place you are assigned to, and the states and localities you may report from.
  • The language you choose in the App.

Your PIN is never stored as written. The server keeps only a one-way hash of it. The App does not ask for your national ID number.

3.2 Your location

The App reads your phone's location only while you use it, at these moments:

  • When you send a field report. The position is sent with the report, and the server uses it to check that the report comes from the locality of its place.
  • When you answer an assessment.
  • When you walk the boundary of a site. The App records your path while you walk, and sends it when you finish.

The App does not track you in the background, and does not read your location when it is closed.

3.3 What you report

  • Field reports: what you write, photos you take or choose, and voice recordings.
  • Short follow-up answers about your place after a report, such as its population or stock level.
  • Answers to assessments, with any photos or recordings you add.
  • The time each item was made and sent.

3.4 Information about other people: households

Workers at gathering sites can register households on the App. For each household the App can collect:

  • The given name and family name of the person registered.
  • Their national ID number, if they have one and agree to give it.
  • Their sex, year of birth and the number of people in the household.
  • The site where they live, filled in from the site the worker is assigned to.

A household belongs to the register of the organisation that recorded it. Another organisation never reads it or searches it. The platform compares a new household with the same organisation's register to find people who may be registered twice. A person decides whether two records are the same household; nothing is merged automatically.

If you register households: tell the people you register what is recorded and why, and record a national ID only when the person agrees. Your organisation is responsible for this information.

3.5 What the App does not collect

  • No advertising identifiers, no advertising and no tracking for advertising.
  • No analytics or usage statistics.
  • No crash reports are sent anywhere.
  • No contacts, call logs, messages or files from your phone, other than the photos you choose to attach.

4. What stays on your phone

  • Your sign-in is kept in the phone's secure storage (Android Keystore), so the App opens signed in.
  • Reports, recordings, follow-up answers and walked boundaries that could not be sent yet are kept in the App's own storage on the phone, and sent when there is a connection. They stay on the phone until they are sent, also if you sign out, so work is not lost. They are deleted from the phone if a different worker signs in on it.

Android keeps the App's storage private to the App. Anyone who can unlock your phone and open the App while you are signed in can see what it shows, so protect your phone with a screen lock.

5. Why we use it

  • To show your organisation, and the people it works with, the situation at the places where it works.
  • To check that reports come from the place they are about.
  • To help organisations plan and coordinate assistance, and avoid registering the same family twice.
  • To sign you in and keep your account secure.

We do not sell any information, and we do not use it for advertising.

6. Who can see it

  • Your organisation sees your reports, your assessments and the households it registered.
  • Other organisations on the platform see places on the map with their status and some figures, which reports and follow-up answers keep up to date. The reports themselves, and the household register, are your organisation's own. Small counts that could point to individual people are withheld from other organisations.
  • UnityHelp's platform administrators can see the platform's data to run and support it.
  • Service providers that run parts of the platform for us, listed in section 7.
  • Authorities only on a valid order of a court with jurisdiction over UnityHelp, Inc. We hand over only what the order names, keep a record, and tell your organisation unless the order forbids it.

8. Where it is kept and how it is protected

  • The platform's servers, database and backups are in Amazon Web Services' Ireland region in the European Union.
  • Everything the App sends travels encrypted over HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or 1.3).
  • Your phone number, and the names and national ID numbers of registered households, are encrypted in the database.
  • Each organisation's household register uses its own key to check national IDs for duplicates, so registers cannot be matched against each other.
  • A sign-in on the App ends after 14 days without use, and after 90 days in any case. Repeated wrong PINs lock the account for a while.
  • Access is limited by role: each account sees what its organisation and role allow.

9. How long it is kept

Reports, assessments and household registers are kept as long as the organisation that recorded them uses the platform, because organisations need their history to plan assistance. When an organisation stops using UnityHelp, or asks us to, its data is deleted from the platform. Copies in our database backups are kept for up to 120 days and then deleted; if something must be removed from the backups sooner, write to info@unityhelp.org. When a field worker's account is closed, the worker can no longer sign in; the reports they sent remain part of their organisation's records unless the organisation asks for them to be deleted.

10. What you can ask for

You can ask to see the information held about you, to correct it, or to have your account and your information deleted. A person whose household was registered can ask the same about their household.

  • Field workers: ask your organisation's administrator, who can correct or close your account. You can also write to info@unityhelp.org.
  • Registered households: ask the organisation that registered you, or write to info@unityhelp.org.

We answer within 14 days, and never later than one month. When we cannot do what is asked, for example because an organisation must keep a record of assistance it gave, we say why.

You can also withdraw the App's permissions in your phone's settings at any time. Without location the App cannot send reports, and without camera or microphone it cannot take photos or recordings.

11. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes we update the date at the top. If a change affects what the App collects, we tell organisations before it takes effect.
